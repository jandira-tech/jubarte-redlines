# Reassessment: skeptical audit of the newly supplied Jubarte reports

## Scope and principal finding

I read all four new files in full, including the historical implementation plan's status banner and unimplemented tests, and compared them with the prior evidence review, revised proposal, core schema, and example documentation. This is an audit of the supplied **reports and their embedded excerpts**. The 114 MB source corpus, its analysis code, the three raw trajectory files, the OpenRouter CSV, the input/output DOCX files, and the judge artifacts named by those reports were not supplied in these four files and were not independently reprocessed here. No repository or previous deliverable was modified.

The new material changes the balance of the diagnosis. It supplies concrete, attributed episodes of navigation overhead, late discovery of unsupported revision interactions, incomplete semantic work despite valid documents, suppressed execution evidence, and a bookmark issue discovered only after a scratch apply. It does **not** establish the reports' broad causal rankings. Two reports contain substantial internal contradictions in the counts used to support those rankings. Several conclusions overstate what a gate, a clean validator, an observational correlation, or a successful dry run can prove.

The strongest correction to our earlier recommendation is that **faithfully applying an authored plan and completing the user's contract instructions are separate problems**. Removing `run_operation` and checking exact desired text still make sense. An empty-change refusal can be an explicit assertion when a caller requires a change, but should no longer be the default proposed cure for non-delivery. None catches the reported runs that successfully made dozens of edits while omitting every scored negotiation requirement. The earlier revised proposal recognizes this in one sentence, but leaves the requirement-checking interface undefined. That gap now deserves more attention than an activation-toggle failure never observed in the supplied experiment reports.

## 1. Evidence tiers and what should change in the earlier review

1. **Directly checkable from the supplied text:** arithmetic, contradictory run lists, quotations' stated contents, shell syntax in examples, the distinction between a proposed test and a test result, and differences between the reports and our prior proposal.
2. **Reported concrete observations:** an identified run received an `ANCHOR_NOT_FOUND`, a cited command piped through `tail`, a plan was applied to scratch, or a judge assigned a particular score. These are useful evidence excerpts, but the present audit cannot confirm that omitted surrounding events do not change their interpretation.
3. **Reported aggregate measurements:** counts across 75 runs, token medians, correlations, and error taxonomy totals. Their independent reliability is limited by absent analysis code and source records; contradictions make some unusable until recomputed.
4. **Interpretations and causal claims:** “the agent understood at every step,” “documentation does not help,” “only this remedy unblocks delivery,” and “the harness explains the failures.” These require substantially more support than chronological anecdotes or unadjusted averages.
5. **Product proposals:** `agent-info`, `locate`, separate check/simulate commands, mandatory authors, persistent IDs, and our PlanV2 changes. None is experimentally evaluated by these reports.

Our prior statement that no raw experiment was supplied remains literally true for the present four-file package, but saying there is now **no case-specific evidence** would be wrong. Update E:22 and P:7 to say that attributed secondary analyses and trace excerpts are available, while the source trajectories and grading files have not been independently audited. The new excerpted cases can substantiate named failure mechanisms, with their provenance stated. They cannot retroactively turn our fourteen disposable release probes into an agent study.

Version separation also remains essential. F2:337–344 shows a persisted help heading identifying **`jubarte 0.12.0-main.d003d8e8`**, whereas the titles repeatedly say 0.12.0. That is not sufficient evidence that these runs used the same binary as our downloaded release, or the audited `0ca2d3e` snapshot. F2:55's `_modified.docx` default also differs from our released-binary probe's default bundle. Require per-run binary digest/build identity and exact help/skill digests before assigning a behavior to a release. The historical plan's banner names `aba48bd5` and dates October 18/19, later than our October 17 baseline; treat its status statements as claims about its named snapshot, not automatically as current audited facts (H:3–48).

## 4. The three-trajectory and telemetry report: useful evidence with overextended conclusions

D is appreciably more careful than F1/F2 about treatment identification. It explicitly says there is no matched vanilla trajectory, no session-level link between the October 7 telemetry and October 16 trajectories, and no model overlap between the two API-key groups (D:5–9, 149–200). Retain those qualifications.

Still, several of its stronger statements need narrowing:

1. **Generation-level inference is not rescued by calling p-values descriptive.** D:190–198 lists Mann–Whitney, bootstrap, Fisher and Wilson methods while acknowledging unknown session clustering. Without independence or a justified sampling model, the quoted CIs and p-values need not have their nominal coverage even for associative population claims. Retain sample medians, empirical distributions and clearly labeled totals; suppress inferential precision or recover session clusters. The large prompt difference is a descriptive fact reported for those two collections, not a treatment effect (D:233–283).
2. **Rubric intervals also require a defensible population.** Four of fifteen or eight of twenty fixed, correlated rubric items are an exact evaluation result, not necessarily independent Bernoulli draws from a population. Wilson intervals and a Fisher comparison across different tasks add little and can mislead (D:343–374). Judge uncertainty needs repeated/adjudicated judgments; task generalization needs a task sample.
3. **The 93-second trajectory is right-censored.** D labels it correctly at 13–17 and 289–307, then repeatedly treats it as a completed failure caused by discovery (335, 427–436, 1035). The defensible observation is “no edit by the last observed event.” Its eventual outcome and reason for missing continuation are unknown. Nineteen Bash calls divided by zero edits is undefined, not a meaningful infinite efficiency metric (715).
4. **More operations do not establish better or more thorough work.** The 95-operation run has far more authoring and QA activity, but its linked grade is 25.23% with eighteen excess redlines (D:21, 35, 339–357). The fast run has no matched grade in the report. “Thorough” should mean observed activity coverage, not superior legal quality. Two completed trajectories do not establish a scaling law for task difficulty (D:21, 311–323).
5. **The result linkage is probabilistic.** D:339 links plan and graded artifact by distinctive provisions/comment wording. That is valuable corroboration, but exact final artifact hashes/session IDs would be stronger. Do not equate its 95-operation case with F1/F2's different named 95-operation zero-score run solely because both have 95 operations.
6. **The bookmark case demonstrates a specific validation-stage gap, not general corruption.** The report says 95 text operations passed dry-run; a scratch output then produced `BOOKMARK_CROSSES_REVISION`; changing after-p33 to before-p34 removed the finding (D:483–528). It calls this a warning, and F2's shown warning returns exit 0 (F2:233–239). We need before/after XML and the validator's severity/coverage to know the actual Word effect. A successful workaround does not identify the precise boundary-transfer bug by itself. Crucially, the audited main `preview_plan` already calls `apply_plan` in memory; it is wrong to generalize that current dry-run only resolves text targets. The missing guarantee is that the complete requested checks run against the exact serialized candidate, not the absence of all simulation. See pinned `src/edit.rs:1685–1695` and `src/bin/jubarte.rs:953–955` below.
7. **It overstates the agent's expectation.** D:528 says the agent naturally expected full structural simulation, while D:624 says it expected scratch application to reveal what dry-run missed. The behavior demonstrates caution and a multistage workflow. It does not uniquely establish that the agent misunderstood dry-run.
8. **In-memory all-or-nothing edit failure is not crash-safe publication.** The wrong-target dry-run wrote nothing by definition. It supports refusal before mutation and consistent source resolution; it does not prove every apply writes atomically, survives I/O failure, or never leaves partial bundles. D:530–560 and 1033 overgeneralize. Our pinned source publication gaps remain relevant (E:95–101; P:44–60).
9. **Count disagreement is not automatically a false count.** Self-reported 214 changes/83 comments versus 199 revision entries/82 comments warrants investigation (D:628–649). Logical replacements, XML carriers, comments, threads, replies, existing marks, and new marks are different units. The report partially acknowledges this; require definitions and baseline-aware counts rather than assuming the grader's measure is the sole truth.
10. **Proposed interfaces are not observed fixes.** “Nearly impossible” targeting errors (D:474–481), “self-healing” errors (821), “highest-value” bootstrap (735), and the claim simulation would eliminate manual XML investigation (859) are hypotheses. Locating an anchor helps but cannot prove the user's intended clause; detecting a bookmark warning still requires a supported repair/explanation. A bootstrap containing the entire schema and document inventory can recreate the initial output overload.

The strongest retained episodes are nevertheless valuable: a wrong paragraph/text pair was refused and repaired; large combined help/read output triggered paging; a scratch apply exposed an extra structural finding; and a mechanically valid edited artifact scored poorly on independent task criteria. These justify specific design work without a vanilla-versus-Jubarte causal comparison.

## 5. Failure mechanisms worth carrying into the revised design

### A. Late discovery of state-dependent editability

The reported refusal names a tracked insertion and asks for a revision decision; the operation schema is then repaired through several failed iterations (F2:116–140, 177–204). The important distinction is between **syntax known in advance**, **document-state constraints knowable before authoring**, and **effects that depend on the proposed replacement**. A generator can supply correct fields and source identities. A source inventory can report protected spans and relevant revision IDs. Only a concrete edit's resolver can decide whether that edit crosses a protected boundary.

Do not replace these with a blanket “paragraph contains revisions, therefore uneditable” flag. An otherwise complex paragraph can contain a safe editable span; conversely, its entire desired text can cross a boundary. Early capability records should state the operation, source range, policy and blocking reason. Actual apply must re-evaluate the computed footprint.

### B. Correct plan execution with incomplete task selection

The named 31-, 35-, and 95-operation zero-score cases are the decisive challenge to our prior emphasis (F2:217–229). Their plans were nonempty. A desired-text compiler could reproduce all selected typographical corrections exactly and still miss every essential requested position. `NO_CHANGES_REQUESTED` only prevents one narrow class of accidental empty application. It cannot become an overall success gate or an incentive to make one trivial change. Prefer an accurately reported unchanged result by default and an explicit `--require-change` assertion when the caller needs it. A requested difference that the engine fails to realize is still a failed postcondition, never an unchanged success.

A separate requirement inventory should trace externally supplied instructions to proposed operations and verified effects, with explicit unresolved/blocked/deferred states. Claims supplied by the same agent are not independently verified simply because they appear in JSON. A deterministic assertion can verify a precise textual or structural predicate; broad legal adequacy needs a separate adjudicator or review. Neither should be labeled by the generic word “verified” without its scope.

### C. Lost execution evidence and wrong artifact identity

The shown `... | tail -n 3; echo "exit=$?"` measures the pipeline's status under its shell configuration, not reliably Jubarte's process status (F2:246–253). Quiet output and later hashes do not establish which requested effect occurred (298–308). The next agent statement even mistakes an actual apply for an upcoming apply.

Preserve direct exit status in the tool harness; keep explicit machine receipts unmuted by quiet; include command phase, source/request/output digests, actual path, artifact role, and checks performed. There is no CLI-only guarantee against a caller discarding both status and output. This is a joint CLI/harness contract, not a reason to change established condition codes arbitrarily.

### D. Source-relative IDs protect batches but complicate successive commits

The wrong p15/p20 pair was safely refused (D:448–472). Snapshot binding and exact anchors should stay. However, repeatedly editing the newly generated tracked output may meet the agent's own insertion boundary, and inserts change positional IDs. Advice to “commit earlier and more often” can increase those problems. Prefer earlier feasibility checks and bounded coherent batches, with explicit resulting identities, rather than a universal early-commit quota.

### E. Preparation stops before serialized integrity checks

The scratch bookmark finding is a reason to apply the existing validator to the exact staged bytes before publication and expose any remaining limitations (D:483–528). It supports our serialized postcondition design but shows text equality alone is insufficient. Reopening the DOCX, checking package/revision/bookmark/comment invariants, and comparing the prepared artifact's source/output correspondence are distinct checks. Validation must report thresholds: a warning is not automatically an invalid file or a required refusal.

### F. Navigation consumes resources while progress is hard to observe

Large help/read material, generated shell filters, and repeated “now drafting” statements are consistent with a navigation bottleneck (F2:98–114, 337–344; D:388–446). They do not prove that reading the full contract was unnecessary. Give agents precise search, bounded typed records, real continuation, relevant schemas, and actionable editability information. Measure whether those reduce **time to a correct, viable plan**, not merely whether they reduce read calls.

| Input paths never modified | **Keep the protection, add a concrete fixed-destination workflow** | The reported task expects `/app/contract.docx`, also the input. A tool that always writes `revised.docx` can miss delivery even when the contents are correct. Use a preserved original baseline as source and explicitly publish to the required task destination, or design a separately authorized, tested in-place transaction. Do not silently broaden `--force`. |
| Full-document generated template with compact receipt | **Revise output grammar and qualify scope** | Require an explicit `-o FILE` or `-o -`; file output returns a compact receipt. That prevents accidental stdout floods, but not the cost when the agent reads the entire file. Prefer scoped generation and bounded file navigation when appropriate; complete contract review can still be required. |
| No separate mandatory dry-run call | **Keep** | The actual executor must run the checks. Adding check, simulate, scratch apply, validate, and final apply as required user steps perpetuates the observed overhead. |
| JSON receipts and preserved exit statuses | **Keep; coordinate with harness** | Quiet plus pipes show why both are needed, but a consumer can discard either. Neither alone proves substantive completion. |

### The missing requirement contract should remain separate from edit intent

A useful next design exercise is a **task assessment artifact**, not another activation flag in every operation. It could identify the source instruction, expected deliverable path and attribution, each requirement's provenance, relevant source targets, operation pointers, and independently checked predicates. Use states such as unassessed, proposed, effect verified, blocked, and not satisfied. Keep mechanical effect verification separate from semantic assessment, and keep reviewer/model claims separate from engine facts.

This avoids reintroducing `run_operation` under a new name such as `requirement_complete:true`. The user or an independent task analyzer defines the requirements; the author proposes edits; the engine verifies authored effects; a separate checker assesses requirements against the actual published artifact. If the author omits an obligation from both plan and self-authored assertion list, merely verifying that list will repeat verification substitution.

A deterministic `--expect` design must define its target, projection, predicate, baseline relation, expected author where relevant, and error/unknown semantics. A keyword appearing in a preserved comment or deleted revision must not satisfy an assertion intended for final operative text. A desired sentence's existence does not prove its legal meaning or consistency with definitions elsewhere. More assertions do not automatically supply a legal oracle.

### Earlier action requires a viable path, not pressure to mutate

The correct product response to late blockers is not “force the agent to edit something early.” That risks shallow typo edits, premature negotiation decisions, or repeated tracked-output editing. Generate source identity and relevant operation contracts early; surface known conditional constraints; let the agent author a coherent bounded request; preview exact effects when helpful; and carry unresolved requirements explicitly. The completion metric should reward correct coverage, not first-write speed or raw operation count.

The same principle limits how far the template should reach. It can remove JSON-field and source-text copying work; it cannot choose legal positions or infer permission to accept another party's revisions. If a required edit is unsupported, a comment-only workaround is not equivalent to implementing a requested body change. Report the blocked requirement and available supported alternatives honestly.

### Selected decisions require a working-baseline guard

The single desired-text design has a sharper risk than simple wrong-ID copying. Suppose a template is generated from the final view of a pending insertion. An author later adds an explicit decision to reject that insertion. If the compiler silently compares the unchanged old template text with the newly rejected working view, the old text looks like a fresh desired rewrite and may reintroduce the rejected words. Source-byte hashing alone cannot detect this: the original source is unchanged, while the decision-dependent working baseline changed. P:354–368 describes this working projection but does not supply a per-row baseline precondition.

Require a generated `before_sha256` on every rewrite row, computed from the exact versioned logical text used as its working basis, and verify it after applying the declared selected decisions in memory. A mismatch requires regenerating/rebasing the row from the new basis; it must not silently reinterpret unchanged old text as new intent. Bind this guard to the target and source/decision context, with explicit serialization rules. It keeps one editable desired `text` field; there is no need to duplicate every baseline paragraph as editable prose. A hash guard reduces accidental mismatch but cannot prove legal intent when two distinct targets share identical text.

Selected decisions should count as requested review effects even when final visible text remains equal—for example, accepting a previously visible insertion changes its revision state. The default unchanged-copy path applies only when there are no requested semantic effects across text, structure, review, comments, and settings. `--require-change` needs that definition too; raw character difference is an inadequate universal change predicate.

Expose scope-specific readiness through `read --for-edit`, and require the same readiness assessment inside template generation and apply. This should make state constraints visible early without forcing a separate prerequisite call for direct application that can assess them itself. Otherwise “mandatory readiness” becomes another ceremony whose token may go stale and whose extra round trip conflicts with the goal of reducing preparatory loops.

## 7. Historical follow-up plan: useful requirements, obsolete implementation details

H's top status banner is the authority for interpreting its historical body. It expressly says paragraph-text recovery, `--nth`, and `--digest` did not ship as written; `pid:` was withdrawn; a specific comment-anchor case landed differently; and move operations did not ship (H:15–44). Its code blocks describe proposed tests, not passing tests. Do not copy the plan's implementation commands or treat future-dated status as proof of behavior at our different audited snapshot.

The useful unresolved needs are bounded failure context, inline occurrence selection, source binding, comment-anchor preservation, and source/output identity mapping. Our previous design already covers most at the contract level. Several old details should not be adopted:

- **First 500 characters is not always corrective context.** The relevant repeated/missing anchor may be near the end of a long clause. H:101–104 and 131–134 cap the paragraph prefix. Prefer context around candidate locations, an explicit truncation indicator, and a supported continuation to exact source text.
- **Eight-character digest prefixes weaken the new binding contract.** H:199–218 proposes a prefix, whereas new saved plans should retain full source digests. A convenience abbreviation must not silently become the same guarantee as a full hash.
- **Native paragraph keys are not a universal durable identity solution.** The body says every Word paragraph has a package-unique key (H:225), yet its own test requires behavior for paragraphs without one (273). The later banner and settled contract provide scoped long IDs and missing-key handling instead. Preserve the agreed ID codec; do not restore `pid`, stamp every paragraph unnecessarily, or trust a global unqualified key lookup.
- **A global `allow_detach` is too blunt for review history.** H:299–350 proposes waiving all comment-anchor removal with one boolean. Prefer explicit affected comment records and supported per-comment dispositions, checked on final serialized output. It must not become a general bypass for disappearing negotiation context.
- **Text containment is an inadequate rewrite anchor-preservation proof.** H:440 proposes approximating survival by whether new text still contains the old anchor text. That can reject a legitimate minimally edited anchor or preserve an unrelated equal substring. Use the resolved change mapping and actual comment markers.
- **The test plan itself has unresolved details.** H:243 expects `pid:HEX`, while line 246 searches a `pid ` form; H:367–407 expects a true tracked move for a one-letter paragraph, while 440 suggests changing the fixture if move detection disagrees. These are reasons to review executable specifications, not instructions to freeze every illustrative expected string.
- **Move support has no strong causal priority in these reports.** It adds a real engine feature and explicitly refuses under keep (H:367, 419). The supplied episodes chiefly concern revision-overlap editing, replies, navigation, semantic omissions, and delivery. Do not let an unrelated six-item historical checklist set the new roadmap.

## 8. Data needed to make the stronger claims reviewable

The useful next analytical product is a reproducible **one-row-per-run ledger**, linked to an event ledger and exact artifacts. It should contain:

1. Run/session/task identifiers; prompt, input DOCX, help, skill, model, harness configuration, binary/build digests; expected party/author and required deliverable path.
2. Scored/unscored status with reason; exact gate predicate and result; judged artifact path and hash; per-item rubric weights/results and judge/version; whether the gate's artifact is the final artifact claimed by the agent.
3. First meaningful read; first edit invocation; first schema-valid plan; first dry run; first successful preparation; first committed effect; final observed event; terminal status and stop/timeout reason. Distinguish time, turns, input tokens, and output budget consumed.
4. Parsed command invocations with shell structure, original Jubarte exit status, stdout/stderr, dry-run/apply distinction, operation count, actual changed effects, and resulting artifact hashes. Do not count a command quoted in help or a here-document as an executed invocation.
5. Error events with code, operation pointer, target/revision state, repeated incident identifier, proposed correction, next action, and whether recovery succeeded. Preserve the original tool/result mapping so joining by `parentUuid` can be checked (F2:348).
6. Documentation exposure with viewed ranges, not just “opened SKILL”; output-persistence events with origin/size and reread links; nudge and truncation classifications; reasons for missing token telemetry.

Then recompute the disputed counts and publish inclusion rules. Investigate gate failures by mutually distinguishable outcomes: no commit, wrong author, direct untracked edits, wrong path, missing artifact, checker mismatch, and genuinely unchanged source. Score planned request coverage separately from execution success. Stratify by scenario and incoming revisions; task families with different rubric counts should not be casually pooled as one identical task.

For product evaluation, use paired or randomized trials with fixed input/task/model/harness and compare the actual interventions: contextual schemas, scoped template authoring, early document-specific constraints, integrated prepared-artifact checks, and receipt/requirement verification. Outcomes should include completed required positions, collateral loss, correct refusals, false completion, viable recovery, and resources—not merely fewer errors or earlier writes. A matched control is necessary to claim reduced failure rates; a successful single recovery is enough to justify a regression fixture for that exact mechanism.

## Conclusion for the parent review

The new evidence supports **reprioritization and a sharper completion model**, not abandonment of the prior interface work. Keep source binding, explicit operations, one desired text field, exact application checks, bounded discovery, honest refusal, and reliable publication receipts. Include tested selective review decisions in the first supported workflow, with working-baseline guards; the prior keep/refuse text-only slice is insufficient for that scope. Strengthen requirement coverage, actual output identity, and staged structural validation, and withdraw default no-effect refusal in favor of truthful unchanged results with an explicit require-change option.

Withdraw the reports' incompatible headline statistics and causal rescue counts until their ledger is repaired. Cite their strongest concrete sequences as reported evidence with filenames and lines. A correctly implemented CLI can still impose avoidable workflow costs; a loud refusal can still be hard to recover from; a mechanically valid document can still omit the user's work. The revised design should expose and measure each of those conditions separately.
